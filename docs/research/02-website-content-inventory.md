## 8. Privacy Policy (`/privacy-policy/`)
TermsFeed-generated template, "Last updated: November 22, 2024". Defines "Country refers to: Poland", refers to user Accounts, purchases and "push notifications", mentions a "Cookies Policy" that does not exist, and gives privacy@flighthoursolution.com as the contact. It does not name the legal entity, the data controller's address, legal bases, retention periods, the processors actually used (hosting in Poland, Elementor forms, Google Fonts loaded from Google, CookieYes) or Swiss FADP rights.

## 10. Copy quality issues (to fix in the rewrite)
- Grammar: "Our services keeps you flying!", "Feel Free To Let Us Now", "prepared of upcoming challenges", "optimized engines management", "consulting for aviation industry" (missing article), "Pratt&Whitney".
- Polish artefacts: skip link "Przejdź do treści", search results page title "Wyniki wyszukiwania", RSS feed titles in Polish, HTML lang `pl-PL`, `og:locale pl_PL`, breadcrumb "Strona główna" in schema.
- Repetition: the same two paragraphs appear on Home and About; "40 years experience" appears five times; the footer contact block appears twice on every page.
- Vague claims without proof: "proven expertise", "seasoned professionals", "decades of experience", "40 years" (combined? per person?), "technology-driven" with no tool named.
- No proof elements at all: no names, photos, bios, client logos, case studies, testimonials, certificates, memberships (SAC/ASA/ISO 9001 are absent), numbers (engines managed, shop visits overseen, savings).
- No news/insights, no downloads, no German version, no Impressum, no Google Map, no LinkedIn URL behind the LinkedIn icon.
- The engine-trading and aircraft-sourcing business visible on LinkedIn is absent.

## 11. Content worth keeping
The Services taxonomy (§4) is the most valuable content on the site: it is specific, uses the industry's own vocabulary (workscope, table inspection, BSI, LLP back-to-birth, maintenance reserves, ECM/EHM, scrap review) and lists concrete engine types. It should survive the rewrite as structured data (service → deliverables → engine families), each item expanded into a short "what you get" description.
