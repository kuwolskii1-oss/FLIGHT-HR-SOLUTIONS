# Flight Hour Solution GmbH — Market context & SEO landscape (September 2026)

Prepared 19 September 2026 to inform the content strategy and copy of the new website for
Flight Hour Solution GmbH (https://flighthoursolution.com), Bahnhofplatz, 6300 Zug, Switzerland.

**Method.** 35 WebSearch / WebFetch calls; 2024–2026 sources, preferring 2025–2026. Every figure
carries its URL and one of three confidence tags:

- **[F]** figure read directly on the fetched page.
- **[S]** figure seen only in a search-result snippet (page paywalled or not fetched within budget).
  Verify against the source before it appears on the website.
- **[V]** vendor / marketing estimate with no traceable primary source. Do not publish as fact;
  at most "industry estimates suggest…", or omit.

Keyword search volumes were **not** measured (no Ahrefs/Semrush access). All target terms are
niche B2B: expect low volume and very high intent. Treat the SERP-competitor lists in section 3
as the real prize: they show who a buyer meets when they search.

## 5. Messaging inputs

### 5.1 Strongest sourced value propositions (each maps to a fact above)

1. **"Engines are more than half of the maintenance bill — we manage the half that matters."**
   Basis: engine share 46 % → 49 % → 53 % (2024–2026) [S] and "biggest, fastest-growing segment" [F].
2. **"A shop visit is a multi-million-dollar purchase. Treat it like one."** Basis: per-visit
   ranges [V], LLP inflation +30 % / repair costs +50–75 % since 2019 [F].
3. **"Independent of OEMs, MROs and lessors."** No conflict with the parties that write the
   invoice; corresponds to what buyers say they need (Acumen, ACC). Competitors who can claim it:
   SGI, TGIS, Alton; many others cannot.
4. **"We plan around the crunch, not against it."** TAT +35 %/+150 %, slot waits +2–6 months,
   demand > supply by ~17 % through 2030 [F].
5. **"Workscope discipline protects value on both sides of the lease."** Reserve/redelivery
   disputes are the top end-of-lease dispute source [F]; early shop visits and module swaps are
   breaking older lease language [F].
6. **"New-gen fluent, legacy proven."** LEAP durability kits (2024–2027) and GTF inspection
   timelines (to 2027–28) alongside CFM56/V2500 volumes staying high through 2028 [S].
7. **"Data-led removal timing."** Independent read of ECM/EHM data; lessors increasingly reward
   demonstrable maintenance quality [F].
8. **"Neutral ground, Swiss discretion."** Swiss neutrality/self-sufficiency tradition [F]; Zug
   as an international base [F].
9. **"Senior people on the job."** 40+ years combined across airline, MRO and OEM roles (site claim).
10. **"For governments: independent oversight where the OEM is the only supplier."** European
    defence budgets doubling; sustainment growth led by engines [F].

### 5.2 Claims to use carefully or avoid

- Do not state specific cost savings ("we save 20 % per shop visit") until you have client-approved
  data; use ranges from real engagements or say "typically a mid-six-figure difference per visit"
  only if true.
- Do not quote SAFE FLY ranges or the 22,000-technician and 15–20 % capacity figures as facts;
  they are unverified vendor/snippet numbers.
- Avoid "world-leading", "one-stop shop", "best-in-class", "synergies", "holistic", "cutting-edge
  AI" — buyers in this niche read them as noise, and the site cannot substantiate them.
- Avoid "Swiss quality" clichés and any wording implying government endorsement or clearance you do
  not hold.
- Avoid "optimized engines management" (current phrasing); use "engine management" / "shop visit
  management" / "powerplant" consistently.
- Do not present the firm as an MRO, a lessor or a parts supplier, and do not use OEM logos.
- Avoid promising slot access you do not control; promise planning and negotiation support.

### 5.3 Tone

Plain, numerate, engineering-literate English (British or US, pick one); German pages in
Swiss-standard German (no ß). Short sentences. Every number with a source, footnoted on the page.
